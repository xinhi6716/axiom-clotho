# Separating area and perimeter in a fixed-state conductance control

Run `python examples/rram-area-perimeter-witness/run.py`. The exact synthetic law is G = 2 + 3A + P. Training rectangles 1×1, 2×2 and 1×4 give (A, P, G) = (1, 4, 9), (4, 8, 22), (4, 10, 24); the design determinant is 6. The same-area shape contrast breaks the ambiguity in this panel. Without one of the shapes there are at most two independent equations for three coefficients; across multiple sizes alone some designs may still be identifiable, so check the actual rank instead of assuming that every experiment must use replicated areas. The held 3×3 rectangle has G = 41. Area-only least squares (with intercept) gives 139/3, error 16/3. The full law gives 41.

A [primary HfOₓ fabrication study](https://pubs.acs.org/aaembp/article/8/12/5090/5115686/A-Design-of-Experiments-Framework-for-Magnetron) reports no significant area dependence for its measured cell resistance. The toy neither revises that observation nor identifies a microscopic current mechanism. In a real shape comparison, preserve process parent, state and sensed voltage, balance measurement order, retain all attempted devices, and require replication at a held geometry under independent process parents. Uncontrolled contact or history can track shape and invalidate a mechanism inference.
